# ADE Human-Machine Framework

## ADE-Core Charter

**Status:** Foundational Draft
**Repository:** ADE-Core
**Version:** 0.2.0

---

## 1. Purpose

The ADE Human-Machine Framework establishes an open architectural foundation for improving the ability of humans and machines to represent, interpret, exchange, and understand information consistently.

ADE-Core provides the foundational concepts, principles, terminology, and architectural relationships upon which the ADE framework and its specialized standards may be developed.

ADE is intended to function as a standards architecture rather than as a proprietary software product or single implementation.

---

## 2. Vision

The vision of ADE is to establish a common architectural foundation through which information can be represented and communicated in ways that are understandable across human, machine, organizational, technological, and temporal contexts.

ADE seeks to reduce ambiguity and fragmentation by establishing common structures for representing meaning, relationships, time, location, events, states, identity, authority, authorization, and other fundamental aspects of information.

---

## 3. Mission

The mission of the ADE Human-Machine Framework is to develop, maintain, and encourage the adoption of open standards that provide a consistent foundation for human and machine understanding.

The framework is intended to support interoperability while allowing independent implementations, technologies, organizations, and communities to participate in its development and use.

---

## 4. Scope

ADE provides an architectural foundation for standards concerned with the representation and communication of information.

The initial scope includes, but is not limited to:

* Human and machine understanding
* Semantic representation
* Temporal representation
* Location representation
* Events and actions
* States and attributes
* Relationships between entities
* Identity and identification
* Authentication and verification
* Authority, capability, and authorization
* Information source and provenance
* Uncertainty, confidence, and assertions
* Information interoperability
* Human-machine communication
* Standards development and conformance

ADE may be extended as new requirements and use cases emerge.

---

## 5. Foundational Principles

ADE-Core is guided by the following principles.

### 5.1 Openness

ADE is intended to support open participation, review, discussion, and development.

### 5.2 Interoperability

ADE standards should enable independent systems and implementations to communicate and interpret information consistently.

### 5.3 Human and Machine Understanding

ADE should consider both human interpretation and machine processing as fundamental requirements.

### 5.4 Extensibility

The architecture should permit additional standards and capabilities to be developed without unnecessarily disrupting the foundational model.

### 5.5 Consistency

Common concepts should have consistent definitions and relationships across ADE standards.

### 5.6 Transparency

The development and evolution of ADE standards should be documented so that decisions and changes can be understood and reviewed.

### 5.7 Technology Neutrality

ADE-Core should establish architectural concepts without requiring dependence on a particular programming language, vendor, platform, or implementation technology.

### 5.8 Evolution

ADE is intended to evolve through documented proposals, technical examination, community discussion, review, and publication.

### 5.9 Context and Proportionality

ADE should support the representation of information according to the context and purpose for which it is required.

Information should not need to be unnecessarily duplicated, exposed, or combined when a reference or limited representation is sufficient to establish the required meaning.

---

## 6. ADE-Core

ADE-Core establishes the common foundation of the ADE Human-Machine Framework.

It provides:

* Foundational concepts
* Core terminology
* Architectural principles
* Relationships between core concepts
* Rules for development of specialized ADE standards
* A foundation for interoperability and conformance

Specialized frameworks should build upon ADE-Core rather than independently redefining fundamental concepts where a common ADE definition already exists.

---

## 7. Core Conceptual Model

ADE-Core establishes a foundational conceptual model consisting of:

1. **Entity**
2. **Object**
3. **Event**
4. **Action**
5. **State**
6. **Attribute**
7. **Time**
8. **Location**
9. **Relationship**
10. **Intent**
11. **Identity**
12. **Authentication**
13. **Authority**
14. **Capability**
15. **Authorization**
16. **Source**
17. **Assertion**
18. **Confidence**
19. **Provenance**

These concepts provide a common vocabulary for describing information, identity, authority, context, and relationships within the ADE architecture.

The definitions and relationships of these concepts will be maintained as formal ADE-Core specifications.

Not every Core concept represents a specialized type of Entity. Some provide semantic context, information about information, identity, authority, or the conditions under which information or actions may be interpreted.

---

## 8. ADE Framework Family

ADE-Core provides the foundation for specialized ADE frameworks.

Initial framework areas include:

### ADE-HTF — Human Timeline Framework

A framework concerned with human-oriented temporal representation and structures.

### ADE-USLF — Universal Semantic Language Framework

A framework concerned with representing meaning and semantic relationships through a common human- and machine-understandable structure.

### ADE-LF — Location Framework

A framework concerned with representing and communicating location across geographic and other reference contexts.

Additional frameworks may be established as the ADE architecture evolves.

---

## 9. Standards Development

ADE standards are intended to develop through an open and documented lifecycle.

The proposed lifecycle is:

**Idea → Discussion → Proposal → Working Draft → Challenge → Review → Decision → Integration → Publication**

The lifecycle may evolve as ADE governance and community processes mature.

---

## 10. Conformance

Implementations claiming conformance with an ADE standard should be evaluated against the requirements of that standard.

Conformance requirements, testing procedures, certification mechanisms, and implementation guidance will be established separately as the ADE framework develops.

---

## 11. Governance

ADE is intended to develop through transparent and documented governance.

Governance structures should support:

* Technical review
* Community participation
* Proposal and change management
* Conflict resolution
* Version control
* Documented decisions
* Preservation of the integrity of published standards

Detailed governance procedures will be developed as the ADE community and standards process mature.

---

## 12. Intellectual Property and Licensing

The intellectual-property and licensing framework for ADE is under development.

Specific licensing terms for ADE specifications, documentation, software, implementations, trademarks, and related materials will be established through appropriate legal and governance processes.

No licensing rights should be inferred beyond those explicitly granted.

---

## 13. Evolution of ADE

ADE-Core is intended to be a living foundation.

Changes to the foundational architecture should be carefully evaluated because changes to ADE-Core may affect multiple standards and implementations.

Proposed changes should therefore be documented, reviewed, and incorporated through the ADE standards-development process.

---

## 14. Status

This document represents the **foundational draft Charter of ADE-Core**.

It is intended to establish the initial architectural direction of the ADE Human-Machine Framework and may be revised through the ADE standards-development process.
